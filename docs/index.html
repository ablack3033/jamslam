JamSlam

Turning noisy iPhone recordings of old-time fiddle jams into sheet music containing only the fiddle melody. This page reports what the pipeline actually produces today, generated from real runs.

Status: not working end to end.

The pipeline runs, exports valid MusicXML, MIDI and ABC, and passes its tests. The musical content is wrong: on most recordings the extracted line collapses onto the tonic and fifth instead of following the fiddle. The scores below are shown as evidence of that, not as results.

What it does

audio -> melody extraction (Basic Pitch, register floor D4, loudest voice)
      -> pitch cleaning -> note segmentation
      -> beat tracking -> quantization
      -> form detection (which stretches repeat) -> consensus across repeats
      -> MusicXML + MIDI + ABC

The bet is consensus: a jam plays each part many times, those passes are independent noisy measurements of one melody, so voting across them should beat any single pass. That bet is not yet testable, because the melody going into it is not the fiddle.

Two findings worth keeping

Form detection was reporting an artifact. A section length that is not commensurate with the tune's period slides its phase forward each block, so blocks an even number apart come back into phase and odd ones do not. Clustering reads that as a clean A/B contrast. Measured on one recording: at block lengths that are multiples of the 16-beat period, similarity is 0.50 flat at every lag; at lengths that are not, it alternates 0.20/0.52 and wins the search outright. Those lengths are no longer generated.

What that exposed is the real blocker. At the correct section length, the A part and the B part resemble each other exactly as much as two passes of the A part do. The extracted line carries the tune's periodicity but not enough detail to separate its sections — a melody-extraction problem that no form prior can fix.

Where the melody is actually lost

Basic Pitch emits per-frame activations and only then thresholds them into discrete notes. Measuring pitch-class entropy at each stage localises the failure precisely:

Thresholding discards 97% of the energy, and what survives is the loudest, most sustained content — guitar and banjo on the tonic and the fifth. Melody selection adds only four further points of concentration, so it is not the culprit. The fiddle is in the recording; it sits below the confidence threshold.

What would move this next

  1. Identify the tune against a catalog, then adapt it. The fiddle is present but weak — only 12.2% of voiced frames peak on anything other than the two dominant pitch classes — so no frame-by-frame rule will find it. Matching a known melody against the un-thresholded posteriorgram integrates that weak evidence across a whole tune. Once the tune is known, what remains is small: find where this performance departs from the known setting.
  2. Ground truth. Eight bars of one recording transcribed by ear would convert every open question here into a measurement. Every number on this page is a proxy, and proxies are gameable. Successful identification would produce this automatically.
  3. Source separation. Removing the bass and guitar stems before extraction attacks the problem at its root. Untested — the pretrained weights were unreachable from the build environment, so this is unproven rather than disproven.
